# PART I: MASTER CONCEPTUAL OUTLINES BY CHAPTER

## CHAPTER 1: PRELIMINARY CONSIDERATIONS

### 1. The Natural Partnership of Law and Philosophy
- **Complementary Dynamic:**
  - **Law:** Provides stability, order, predictability, codified rules, and sovereign coercive enforcement.
  - **Philosophy:** Inquisitive by nature; questions unexamined assumptions; inquires into the telos (purpose), ethical basis, and moral limits of legal authority.
  - **Synthesis:** Law without philosophy degenerates into mechanical tyranny or empty formalism; philosophy without law remains an abstract academic exercise without social efficacy.

### 2. The Letter vs. The Spirit of the Law
- **The Letter of the Law (*Litera Legis*):** The literal, grammatical, surface wording of a statutory or constitutional provision.
- **The Spirit of the Law (*Ratio Legis / Anima Legis*):** The underlying intent, purpose, meaning, and socio-economic reason that motivated the lawmaker to enact the statute.
- **The Golden Canon:** *"The spirit, rather than the letter of a statute determines its construction."* What is within the spirit is within the statute even if not within the letter; what is within the letter but not within the spirit is excluded (*Alonzo v. IAC*, *League of Cities v. COMELEC*, *DCCCO v. CIR*).
- **Biblical & Jurisprudential Root:** *"The letter that killeth vs. the spirit that vivifieth"* (2 Corinthians 3:6). Courts must refuse mechanical application when it defeats legislative intent and produces injustice.

### 3. Historical Roots of Philippine Public Law
- **Civil Law Foundation:** Substantive private law (Civil Code and Penal Code) inherited from Spain, rooted in Roman Law (Justinian's *Corpus Juris Civilis*).
- **American Public Law Transplantation:** Constitutional law, the Bill of Rights, separation of powers, judicial review, administrative law, and corporate law transplanted under American sovereignty (McKinley's Instructions, Phil. Bill of 1902, Jones Law, 1935 Constitution).

### 4. Separation of Powers and Checks and Balances
- **The Classical Trichotomy:** Governmental power is distributed among three coordinate, independent, and co-equal departments:
  1. **Executive:** Vested in the President; executes and applies the law (*Villena v. Secretary of the Interior*).
  2. **Legislative:** Vested in Congress (Senate and House of Representatives); prescribes general rules of conduct for society.
  3. **Judiciary:** Vested in the Supreme Court and statutory lower courts; interprets the law and decides actual controversies.
- **Checks and Balances:** Prevents concentration of despotic power. The President exercises veto power; Congress exercises oversight, budgetary control, and confirmation; the Judiciary exercises judicial review.
- **Doctrine of Qualified Political Agency (Alter Ego Doctrine):** Enunciated by Justice Laurel in *Villena v. Secretary of the Interior* (67 Phil. 464). Acts performed by Department Secretaries in the regular course of business are presumptively the acts of the Chief Executive unless disapproved or reprobated by the President.

### 5. Judicial Power, Judicial Supremacy, and Judicial Review
- **Definition of Judicial Power (Art. VIII, Sec. 1, 1987 Constitution):**
  1. *Traditional Jurisdiction:* Settle actual controversies involving rights legally demandable and enforceable.
  2. *Expanded Certiorari Jurisdiction:* Determine whether there has been grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction on the part of any branch or instrumentality of government (abolishing the unreviewable political question shield in human rights and constitutional violations).
- **Doctrine of Judicial Supremacy (*Angara v. Electoral Commission*, 63 Phil. 139):** When the Supreme Court strikes down an unconstitutional act of Congress or the Executive, it does not claim superiority over the political branches. It merely asserts the supremacy of the Constitution as the supreme covenant ordained by the people.
- **The Four Inviolable Requisites of Judicial Review:**
  1. **Actual Case or Controversy:** Concrete legal conflict between adverse parties admitting of specific judicial relief, not a moot, hypothetical, or advisory opinion.
  2. **Legal Standing (*Locus Standi*):** Personal and substantial interest; direct, actual, or imminent injury. (Relaxed in cases of transcendental importance: *Kilosbayan v. Guingona*).
  3. **Earliest Opportunity:** Pleaded in the initial court proceedings, unless jurisdictional or fundamental.
  4. **Lis Mota:** The constitutional question must be the very pivot or deciding issue without which the dispute cannot be resolved.

### 6. The Three Independent Constitutional Commissions
- **The Triad:** Civil Service Commission (CSC), Commission on Elections (COMELEC), and Commission on Audit (COA).
- **Institutional Independence:** Ensured through: (a) constitutional creation; (b) fiscal autonomy; (c) fixed staggered terms; (d) compensation cannot be decreased during tenure; (e) removal only by impeachment; (f) rule-making powers.
- **Scope of Powers:**
  - **CSC:** Central personnel agency; upholds merit and fitness; *cannot substitute its judgment for the appointing authority* (*Luego v. CSC*, *Lapinid v. CSC*).
  - **COMELEC:** Enforces and administers election laws; exclusive jurisdiction over election offenses (*Gallardo v. Tabamo*).
  - **COA:** Constitutional watchdog over public funds; examines, audits, and disallows illegal, irregular, or excessive expenditures (*Dingcong v. Guingona*).

## CHAPTER 2: THE GREAT SCHOOLS OF JURISPRUDENCE

### 1. Theories of Criminal Law
- **The Classical / Juristic School:**
  - Man is an intelligent moral agent possessing absolute free will.
  - Accountability is retributive; punishment is calibrated strictly to the objective gravity of the crime committed.
- **The Positivist / Realistic School:**
  - Pioneered by Lombroso, Garofalo, and Ferri.
  - Rejects free will; criminal acts are the product of psychological, biological, and social pathology.
  - Purpose of penalty is curative, rehabilitative, preventive, and social defense.

### 2. Natural Law Jurisprudence (*Jus Naturale*)
- **Core Tenet:** Law is rooted in eternal moral principles, human nature, and divine reason. An unjust law is not true law (*Lex iniusta non est lex*).
- **Four Historical Phases:**
  1. *Classical Greek/Roman Antiquity:* Socrates (moral inquiry), Plato (ideal justice & philosopher king), Aristotle (general justice & human telos), Cicero (*Right reason in agreement with nature*).
  2. *Scholastic / Thomistic Phase:* St. Thomas Aquinas (*Summa Theologiae*). Four tiers of law: Eternal Law, Natural Law (man's rational participation in eternal law), Divine Law (revealed), and Human Law. Primary precept: *Good is to be done and pursued, and evil avoided*. Five natural inclinations: self-preservation, procreation/rearing of offspring, living in society, knowing truth, and worshiping God.
  3. *Enlightenment & Modern Phase:* Grotius, Locke, Rousseau. Natural rights (life, liberty, property) as the foundation of civil society and social contract.
  4. *International Law Phase:* Common moral law of nations; universal human rights treaties binding even during constitutional interregnums (*Republic v. Sandiganbayan*).

### 3. Legal Positivism
- **Core Tenet:** Law is purely a man-made social construct. The validity of a legal norm depends on its institutional source, not its moral content.
- **The Three Pillars (Wrenn formulation):**
  1. *The Social Fact Thesis:* Legal validity is determined by social facts of sovereign enactment or recognition.
  2. *The Conventionality Thesis:* Validating social facts derive authority from conventional agreement among legal officials.
  3. *The Separability Thesis:* Law and morality are conceptually distinct. Law as it *is* is strictly separated from law as it *ought to be*.
- **Key Thinkers:**
  - *John Austin:* Command Theory of Law (command of sovereign + threat of sanction + habit of obedience). Criticized Blackstone's naturalism as an invitation to anarchy.
  - *H.L.A. Hart:* Union of Primary and Secondary Rules; the *Rule of Recognition* as the supreme empirical criterion of legal validity.
  - *Hans Kelsen:* Pure Theory of Law (*Reine Rechtslehre*). Methodological purification of law from sociology, ethics, and politics. Law as a normative hierarchy (*Stufenbau*) of 'ought' (*Sollen*) statements, capped by the hypothetical *Grundnorm* (Basic Norm).

### 4. Legal Realism
- **Core Tenet:** Revolt against mechanical formalism. Law is not a static set of logical deduction rules, but the actual living behavior, psychology, and decisions of judges and legal officials.
- **Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.:**
  - *"The life of the law has not been logic: it has been experience."*
  - **The Bad Man Theory:** To know the law, look through the eyes of the bad man who cares only for the actual judicial and coercive consequences of his conduct (*The Path of the Law*).
- **The Operative Fact Doctrine (*Municipality of Malabang v. Benito*):** Replaces the rigid fiction of *Norton v. Shelby County* (that an unconstitutional law never existed) with judicial realism: an unconstitutional law was an operative historical fact that produced reliance, rights, and real-world transactions that cannot be magically undone.
- **Stare Decisis in Constitutional Law (*Lambino v. COMELEC* & The *Casey* Prongs):** Precedent must yield when proven unworkable, when reliance is minimal, when legal doctrines have evolved, or when facts have fundamentally changed.

### 5. Ronald Dworkin's Interpretivism / Constructivism
- **Core Tenet:** Rejects both Positivism and Naturalism. Law is not merely explicit rules, nor is it external natural law. It consists of **Rules** and **Principles**.
- **Principles vs. Rules:**
  - *Rules* apply in an all-or-nothing fashion.
  - *Principles* (e.g., *"No person shall profit from their own wrong"*) have weight, express moral fairness, and govern when rules run out.
- **Rights as Trumps:** Fundamental individual rights trump utilitarian collective goals.
- **Hard Cases & Law as Integrity:** Judges must construct the best moral and political theory that fits and justifies the community's legal tradition (*Salvacion v. Central Bank*).

### 6. Sociological Jurisprudence & The Historical School
- **Sociological Jurisprudence:**
  - *Eugen Ehrlich:* The **Living Law** (inner order of social associations) precedes state statutory codes.
  - *Roscoe Pound:* **Social Engineering**; law balances conflicting Individual, Public, and Social Interests with minimum friction.
- **The Historical School:**
  - *Friedrich Carl von Savigny:* Law is not artificially drafted by legislatures; it grows naturally out of the **Volksgeist** (the common consciousness and historical spirit of the nation).

## CHAPTER 3: LAW, THE STATE, AND GOVERNANCE

### 1. The State, The Polis, and Governance
- **Aristotle's Teleology:** The State is the highest form of human community, aiming at the supreme good (virtue, happiness, eudaimonia).
- **The Four Elements of the State:**
  1. *People:* An aggregate of individuals bound by allegiance. In constitutional law, "the people" refers to qualified citizens acting through formal constitutional channels (*Javellana v. Executive Secretary*).
  2. *Territory:* Terrestrial, fluvial, aerial, and maritime domains under sovereign jurisdiction.
  3. *Government:* The political machinery or institution by which the State makes and enforces its will (*US v. Dorr*).
  4. *Sovereignty:* The supreme, permanent, and unconditional power of self-determination.
- **Functions of Government (*Bacani v. NACOCO*):**
  - *Constituent Functions:* Indispensable bonds of society (defense, order, justice, political relations).
  - *Ministrant Functions:* Optional, promotional social/commercial welfare functions.
- **Doctrine of Parens Patriae:** The State acts as the sovereign guardian of the vulnerable (minors, disabled, indigent) (*Southern Luzon Drug Corp. v. DSWD*).

### 2. De Jure vs. De Facto Governments
- **De Jure:** Legitimate title under the constitution, but may lack physical control.
- **De Facto:** Actual physical control without regular legal title.
- **Three Types of De Facto Governments (*Co Kim Cham v. Valdez Tan Keh*):**
  1. *Revolution / Usurpation:* Insurgents overthrow lawful government.
  2. *Insurrection / Secession:* Rebels sever territory from parent state.
  3. *Belligerent Occupation:* Enemy military force occupies territory during wartime. Judicial acts of a non-political nature survive restoration of the de jure sovereign.

### 3. The Three Inherent Powers of the State
- **Congenital and Primordial:** Precede the constitution; essential for self-preservation.
  1. **Police Power:** Regulates liberty and property for general welfare (*Salus populi est suprema lex*). No compensation required (*Sangalang v. IAC*).
  2. **Power of Eminent Domain:** Taking of private property for public use upon payment of *just compensation* (full and fair market equivalent at the time of taking, determined under a willing buyer-willing seller standard) (*Republic v. Ortigas*, *Republic v. PNB*).
  3. **Power of Taxation:** The lifeblood of the government (*CIR v. Algue*). Based on the necessity theory and the symbiotic benefits-received relationship between State and citizen.

### 4. Sovereignty and State Immunity from Suit
- **Constitutional Maxim:** *"The State may not be sued without its consent"* (Art. XVI, Sec. 3).
- **Philosophical Ground:** *Par in parem non habet imperium* (Equals have no sovereign authority over each other). Public funds cannot be diverted to private litigation.
- **The Restrictive Theory of State Immunity:**
  - *Acts Jure Imperii (Sovereign/Governmental Acts):* Immunity applies (*Sanders v. Veridiano II*, *Arigo v. Swift*).
  - *Acts Jure Gestionis (Commercial/Proprietary Acts):* Immunity is waived; state acts as a private merchant.
- **Waiver of Immunity:** Must be express (statute) or implied (State initiates suit or enters commercial venture). Always strictly construed (*Baer v. Tizon*).

### 5. Constitutionalism & Rules of Construction
- **Verba Legis (Plain Meaning Rule):** Words must be given their ordinary and natural meaning (*Regalado v. Yulo*, *Tecson v. COMELEC*).
- **Ratio Legis Est Anima:** In ambiguity, look to the intent of the framers and ratifiers (*Francisco v. House of Representatives*).
- **Ut Magis Valeat Quam Pereat:** The Constitution must be construed as a consistent, harmonious whole (*Casela v. CA*).
- **Self-Executing Provisions (*Manila Prince Hotel v. GSIS*):** Constitutional provisions are presumed complete and judicially enforceable without legislative intervention.

### 6. The Rule of Law, Judicial Duty, and Social Justice
- **Rule of Law vs. Government of Men (*Villavicencio v. Lukban*):** No official is above the law. Executive benevolence cannot override the Great Writ of Habeas Corpus and due process.
- **Judicial Duty vs. Moral Conscience (*People v. Veneracion*):** A judge cannot substitute his personal religious beliefs for positive statutory penalties.
- **Public Office as a Public Trust (Art. XI, Sec. 1):** High ethical standards for civil servants and judges (*Funa v. Duque III*).
- **Social Justice (*Guido v. RPA*):** Social justice is equality of opportunity, fairness, and human dignity—not the arbitrary atomization or confiscation of small private lands.

# PART III: MASTER COMPENDIUM OF LANDMARK CASE DIGESTS

### 1. Alonzo v. Intermediate Appellate Court, 150 SCRA 259 (1987)
- **Ponente:** Justice Isagani Cruz
- **Core Subject:** Statutory Construction; Spirit of the Law vs. Letter of the Law; Legal Redemption.
- **Facts:** Five siblings inherited a parcel of land in equal undivided shares. In 1963 and 1964, two siblings sold their respective shares to spouses Alonzo. The buyers immediately took possession, built a house, and cultivated the property in plain sight of the other co-heirs. In 1976—more than a decade later—the remaining co-heirs filed an action for legal redemption under Article 1088 of the Civil Code, which provides that a co-heir may redeem a share sold to a stranger within 30 days *from notice in writing by the vendor*. The co-heirs claimed that since no formal written notice was ever delivered, the 30-day period had not yet run.
- **Issue:** May co-heirs exercise the right of legal redemption after thirteen years of undisputed actual knowledge of the sale, on the ground that the statutory requirement of *written* notice was not strictly observed?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** The Supreme Court held that actual knowledge satisfied the statutory requirement.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  > *"While we admittedly may not legislate, we nevertheless have the power to interpret the law in such a way as to reflect the will of the legislature. While we may not read into the law a purpose that is not there, we nevertheless have the right to read out of it the reason for its enactment. In doing so, we defer not to the letter that killeth but to the spirit that vivifieth."*
  The purpose of written notice is simply to ensure that the co-heirs are informed of the sale. When actual knowledge has existed for over a decade, insisting on a written document would make the statute an instrument of extortion and injustice.

### 2. League of Cities of the Philippines (LCP) v. COMELEC, 608 SCRA 636 (2009)
- **Core Subject:** Uniform Criteria for Cityhood; Statutory Exemption Clauses; Substantial Justice.
- **Facts:** Congress enacted RA 9009 amending Section 450 of the Local Government Code (RA 7160) by increasing the income requirement for conversion into a component city from Php 20 million to Php 100 million. During debates, it was recognized that 16 municipalities had cityhood bills pending before the increase. Congress subsequently passed 16 individual Cityhood Laws exempting these specific municipalities from the Php 100 million threshold. The League of Cities challenged the 16 Cityhood Laws as unconstitutional violations of Section 10, Article X (which requires criteria to be embodied in the Local Government Code itself) and the Equal Protection Clause.
- **Issue:** Are the 16 Cityhood Laws unconstitutional for creating exemptions not found in the text of the Local Government Code?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** The Cityhood Laws are constitutional.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Congress explicitly intended to exempt the 16 municipalities whose applications were caught mid-stream. Legislative intent is the soul of the statute. The 16 municipalities formed a valid distinct class because their applications were pending prior to the enactment of RA 9009. The Court reaffirmed its inherent power to suspend its own procedural rules to achieve substantial justice and give effect to legislative will.

### 3. Dumaguete Cathedral Credit Cooperative (DCCCO) v. CIR, 610 SCRA 652 (2010)
- **Core Subject:** Tax Exemption of Cooperatives; Ejusdem Generis; Teleological Construction.
- **Facts:** The BIR assessed DCCCO for deficiency withholding taxes on interest paid to its members on savings and time deposits under Section 24(B)(1) of the NIRC, which imposes a 20% final withholding tax on interest from bank deposits, deposit substitutes, trust funds, and "similar arrangements." DCCCO claimed exemption under the Cooperative Code (RA 6938).
- **Issue:** Are credit cooperatives legally required to withhold final income tax on interest earned by members on their deposits?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** Credit cooperatives are exempt.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Under the rule of *ejusdem generis*, "similar arrangements" refers only to banking arrangements. Cooperatives are self-help economic organizations, not banks. Under Articles 61 and 62 of RA 6938, the legislative intent is to grant cooperatives preferential tax treatment to foster capital accumulation among small producers. Tax exemptions for social justice institutions must be interpreted in light of their teleological legislative intent.

### 4. Republic v. Sandiganbayan, 407 SCRA 10 (2003)
- **Core Subject:** The 1986 EDSA Interregnum; Exclusionary Rule; Natural Law Basis of Human Rights.
- **Facts:** During the revolutionary interregnum following the 1986 EDSA Revolution, the PCGG and an AFP Anti-Graft Board raided the home of Elizabeth Dimaano (confidential secretary of Gen. Fabian Ver) and seized millions in cash, firearms, and assets without a search warrant. Dimaano moved to suppress the seized items under the Exclusionary Rule.
- **Issue:** Did the Bill of Rights (specifically against unreasonable searches and seizures) apply during the revolutionary interregnum when the 1973 Constitution was abrogated and before the 1986 Provisional Freedom Constitution was adopted?
- **Ruling:** The majority held the 1973 Constitution was abrogated during the revolutionary interregnum, and the revolutionary government operated without constitutional limitations. However, under international law covenants (UDHR, ICCPR), basic human rights remained binding.
- **Justice Puno's Natural Law Doctrine (Separate Opinion):**
  Human rights are not conferred by constitutional charters; they are *natural rights* inherent in human dignity. Even when positive constitutional charters are abrogated by revolution, rulers remain morally and legally bound by natural law and the fundamental precepts of human rights.

### 5. Angara v. Electoral Commission, 63 Phil. 139 (1936)
- **Ponente:** Justice Jose P. Laurel
- **Core Subject:** Separation of Powers; Judicial Supremacy; Inherent Powers of Constitutional Bodies.
- **Facts:** Jose Angara was declared winner of a seat in the National Assembly. Pedro Ynsua filed an election protest before the Electoral Commission after the National Assembly had already confirmed the election results. Angara petitioned for prohibition, arguing that the National Assembly had exclusive authority to fix the deadline for election protests, and the Electoral Commission had no jurisdiction.
- **Issue:** Does the Supreme Court have the constitutional power to arbitrate the boundary of jurisdiction between the National Assembly and the Electoral Commission?
- **Ruling:** **YES.** The Electoral Commission had exclusive constitutional authority to decide election protests.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  *The Doctrine of Judicial Supremacy:* In a tripartite constitutional democracy, the Constitution sets forth the boundaries of governmental power. The judiciary is the ordained arbiter of constitutional conflicts. When the Court exercises judicial review, it does not assert superiority over the political branches; it merely asserts the supremacy of the Constitution.

### 6. Villena v. Secretary of the Interior, 67 Phil. 464 (1939)
- **Ponente:** Justice Jose P. Laurel
- **Core Subject:** Executive Power; The Doctrine of Qualified Political Agency (Alter Ego Doctrine).
- **Facts:** The Secretary of the Interior initiated an administrative investigation against Mayor Villena of Makati and suspended him from office. Villena challenged the suspension, arguing that under the Constitution, the power to investigate and suspend municipal mayors belonged exclusively to the President, and the Secretary had no statutory authority to act on his own.
- **Issue:** May a Cabinet Secretary exercise presidential supervisory and disciplinary authority without an express written directive from the President?
- **Ruling:** **YES.** The suspension was valid.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  *The Alter Ego Doctrine:* The President cannot be expected to personally attend to every single administrative detail of governance. The heads of the executive departments are the President's confidential advisers and alter egos. Their official acts, performed in the regular course of business, are presumptively the acts of the Chief Executive, unless disapproved or reprobated by the President.

### 7. Luego v. Civil Service Commission, 143 SCRA 327 (1986)
- **Ponente:** Justice Isagani Cruz
- **Core Subject:** Civil Service; Power of Appointment vs. Power of Approval.
- **Facts:** Luego was appointed Administrative Officer II by the City Mayor on a permanent basis. A competing applicant protested before the CSC. The CSC revoked Luego's appointment and directed the appointment of the rival candidate on the ground that the rival had superior qualifications.
- **Issue:** Does the Civil Service Commission have the authority to revoke a valid permanent appointment and direct the appointment of another person whom it considers better qualified?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** The CSC's order was null and void.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  The power of appointment is an essentially discretionary executive function vested exclusively in the appointing authority. The CSC's authority is strictly limited to verifying whether the appointee possesses the minimum statutory qualifications. If the appointee is qualified, the CSC has the ministerial duty to approve the appointment. The CSC cannot substitute its judgment for that of the appointing officer by choosing who is "better qualified."

---

### 8. Lapinid v. Civil Service Commission, 197 SCRA 106 (1991)
- **Core Subject:** Reaffirmation of Luego; Limits of CSC Discretion.
- **Facts:** Despite the clear ruling in *Luego*, the CSC continued to revoke appointments made by department heads whenever it believed an aggrieved rival was more experienced or had higher ratings.
- **Issue:** May the CSC disregard the *Luego* doctrine and assert authority to replace appointees on subjective evaluations of merit?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** The Supreme Court severely admonished the CSC.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  The Court emphatically warned the CSC that decisions of the Supreme Court form part of the legal system and bind all lower bodies. The CSC has no administrative power to overturn judicial precedents or encroach upon the discretion of appointing authorities.

---

### 9. Gallardo v. Tabamo, 218 SCRA 253 (1993)
- **Core Subject:** COMELEC Jurisdiction; Exclusive Authority over Election Prohibitions.
- **Facts:** In the run-up to the May 1992 elections, an RTC issued an injunction restraining Governor Gallardo from continuing public works projects, alleging that they violated the Omnibus Election Code ban against the disbursement of public funds during the campaign period.
- **Issue:** Does a Regional Trial Court have jurisdiction to issue an injunction enforcing election bans against public works disbursements?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** RTC has no jurisdiction.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  The 1987 Constitution envisions a fully independent Commission on Elections. Exclusive jurisdiction over the enforcement of election laws, including the prohibition against public funds disbursements during the election campaign period, belongs exclusively to the COMELEC. RTC injunctions interfering with election administration constitute grave abuse of discretion.

---

### 10. Mobil Oil Philippines, Inc. v. Diocares, 29 SCRA 656 (1969)
- **Ponente:** Justice Enrique Fernando
- **Core Subject:** Natural Law Foundation of Contracts; Promise as Moral Binding Force.
- **Facts:** Diocares executed an unrecorded real estate mortgage to secure a commercial loan from Mobil Oil. Upon default, Mobil Oil moved to foreclose. The trial court dismissed the foreclosure on the ground that under Article 2125 of the Civil Code, recording is indispensable for the constitution of a mortgage.
- **Issue:** Is an unrecorded real estate mortgage legally binding between the contracting parties?
- **Ruling:** **YES.** The mortgage is valid between the parties.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  While recording is necessary to bind third persons, an unrecorded contract binds the immediate parties. Tracing the doctrine to natural law, Justice Fernando held that a contract is rooted in the moral obligation of a promise (*pacta sunt servanda*). Law elevates the moral duty of fidelity into an enforceable legal right.

---

### 11. Gomez v. Palomar, 25 SCRA 827 (1968)
- **Core Subject:** Police Power; Legal Realism; Presumption of Constitutionality; Equal Protection.
- **Facts:** Republic Act No. 1635 required the affixing of a 5-centavo anti-TB stamp on all mail during the annual anti-tuberculosis fund campaign. Petitioner argued that the law violated the Equal Protection Clause because it arbitrarily singled out users of the postal system to fund a general public disease.
- **Issue:** Does the mandatory anti-TB stamp violate the Equal Protection Clause?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** The law is constitutional.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Judicial realism demands that the legislature be given wide latitude in classifying subjects for social welfare taxation and police regulations. Eradicating tuberculosis is a paramount public welfare goal. Mail users form a legitimate class of citizens utilizing government communication facilities who may reasonably be called upon to support a public health campaign.

---

### 12. Municipality of Malabang v. Benito, 27 SCRA 533 (1969)
- **Ponente:** Justice Querube Makalintal
- **Core Subject:** Legal Realism; The Doctrine of Operative Fact; Void Ab Initio Doctrine Rejected.
- **Facts:** President Garcia issued EO 386 creating the Municipality of Balabagan pursuant to Section 68 of the Revised Administrative Code. Years later, in *Pelaez v. Auditor General*, Section 68 was declared unconstitutional because creating municipalities is an exclusively legislative power. Petitioners sought to invalidate Balabagan and nullify all acts performed by its officials.
- **Issue:** Does the declaration of unconstitutionality wipe out all legal existence and past acts of the municipality as though it never existed (*Norton v. Shelby County*), or must the court apply the *Doctrine of Operative Fact*?
- **Ruling:** Balabagan was declared without legal existence, but its prior acts were protected under the **Doctrine of Operative Fact**.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  *The Operative Fact Doctrine:* The actual existence of a statute or executive order prior to judicial invalidation is an undeniable historical and operative fact. Contracts were made, taxes collected, and public rights created. The past cannot be erased. Courts must realistically recognize these operative facts to prevent widespread legal chaos. Balabagan operated as a de facto corporation during its existence.

---

### 13. Lambino v. Commission on Elections, 505 SCRA 160 (2006)
- **Core Subject:** Constitutional Amendment vs. Revision; Stare Decisis; The Casey Framework.
- **Facts:** The Lambino Group gathered signatures to amend the 1987 Constitution via People's Initiative, proposing to shift from a bicameral presidential system to a unicameral parliamentary system. The petition was rejected by the COMELEC based on *Santiago v. COMELEC* (which held RA 6735 was inadequate for initiative). Lambino asked the Supreme Court to overrule *Santiago*.
- **Issue:** Should the Supreme Court overrule its precedent in *Santiago v. COMELEC* under the doctrine of *stare decisis*?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** Precedent was maintained.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  The Court adopted the four-prong prudential framework from *Planned Parenthood v. Casey* (505 U.S. 833): (1) practical workability; (2) reliance; (3) doctrinal development; (4) factual changes. None of the factors supported abandoning precedent. Furthermore, shifting to a parliamentary system is an unconstitutional *revision*, not a mere *amendment*, and cannot be undertaken via People's Initiative.

---

### 14. Salvacion v. Central Bank of the Philippines, 278 SCRA 27 (1997)
- **Ponente:** Justice Justo P. Torres
- **Core Subject:** Ronald Dworkin's Interpretivism; Foreign Currency Deposit Act (RA 6426); Equity over Form.
- **Facts:** Karen Salvacion, a 12-year-old Filipina child, was abducted and brutally raped over four days by Greg Bartelli, an American tourist. The trial court awarded Salvacion Php 1 million in civil damages. To satisfy the judgment, garnishment was issued against Bartelli's dollar deposit in China Banking Corporation. China Bank and the Central Bank refused, citing Section 8 of RA 6426, which explicitly provides that foreign currency deposits are exempt from attachment, garnishment, or court process.
- **Issue:** May an absolute statutory exemption from garnishment be invoked to prevent a child rape victim from collecting civil indemnity from a foreign criminal's bank account?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** China Bank was ordered to release the dollar deposits.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  The Court denounced the literal application of the statute as legal tyranny: *"In the fine print of terms, the exemption was designed to encourage foreign depositors to invest foreign currency in the Philippine banking system... It was never intended to establish a safe haven for foreign criminals to mock our penal laws and evade civil liability to victims of their heinous crimes."* Justice, equity, and human dignity prevail over technical statutory immunities.

---

### 15. People v. Acierto, 92 Phil. 534 (1953)
- **Ponente:** Justice Pedro Tuason
- **Core Subject:** Constructivist Statutory Interpretation; Military Bases Jurisdiction.
- **Facts:** Acierto, a Filipino civilian contractor working inside a U.S. military base in the Philippines, committed estafa by falsifying payroll records. He claimed that under the RP-US Military Bases Agreement, exclusive jurisdiction over offenses committed inside the base belonged to U.S. military courts.
- **Issue:** Do Philippine domestic courts possess jurisdiction over offenses committed inside a foreign military base by a civilian contractor?
- **Ruling:** **YES.** Philippine courts have jurisdiction.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Treaties granting foreign extraterritorial jurisdiction must be strictly construed to preserve national territorial sovereignty. A civilian contractor is not an enlisted member of the armed forces. Constructivist interpretation requires that exceptional military exemptions be confined strictly to their military defensive purpose.

---

### 16. United States v. Dorr, 2 Phil. 269 (1903)
- **Core Subject:** Elements of the State; Definition of Government vs. Individual Officers; Sedition.
- **Facts:** Editors of the *Manila Freedom* were convicted of criminal libel and sedition under Act No. 292 for publishing an article harshly condemning the Civil Commission and alleging corrupt appointments.
- **Issue:** Does a defamatory critique of individual government officials constitute criminal libel against the "Government" itself?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** Conviction reversed.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  The term "Government" refers to the institution, the political system, or the aggregate of governing departments, not the mortal individuals who temporarily hold public office. Free speech permits rigorous, even vehement criticism of individual officers without being branded sedition against the government.

---

### 17. Bacani and Matoto v. National Coconut Corporation (NACOCO), 100 Phil. 468 (1956)
- **Ponente:** Justice Felix Bautista Angelo
- **Core Subject:** Constituent vs. Ministrant Functions of Government; Status of GOCCs.
- **Facts:** Plaintiffs obtained a judgment against NACOCO. Court stenographers sought payment of transcript fees. NACOCO claimed exemption under Section 16, Rule 130, arguing that as a government entity, it was part of the "Government of the Republic of the Philippines."
- **Issue:** Does a government-owned corporation performing proprietary functions form part of the "Government" for purposes of statutory fee exemptions?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** NACOCO must pay stenographic fees.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Adopting Woodrow Wilson's classification, the Court distinguished between:
  1. *Constituent Functions:* Compulsory, sovereign functions indispensable for civil order (defense, justice, peace, political relations).
  2. *Ministrant Functions:* Optional, commercial, and promotional functions undertaken for general prosperity.
  NACOCO was engaged in purely ministrant/proprietary functions. When the government establishes an independent corporation for commercial trade, it sheds its sovereign immunity and status.

---

### 18. Co Kim Cham v. Valdez Tan Keh and Dizon, 75 Phil. 113 (1945)
- **Ponente:** Justice Antonio Horrilleno
- **Core Subject:** De Facto Governments; Belligerent Military Occupation; Validity of Judicial Acts.
- **Facts:** Following the liberation of the Philippines, petitioner sought to invalidate court proceedings and judgments rendered during the Japanese occupation, asserting they were null and void because the Japanese-sponsored government was an illegal de facto regime.
- **Issue:** Are judicial decisions of non-political nature rendered by domestic courts during a belligerent foreign military occupation valid following the restoration of the de jure government?
- **Ruling:** **YES.** Judicial proceedings remain valid.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Under the Hague Conventions of 1907 and international law, the Japanese occupation government was a **De Facto Government of Belligerent Military Occupation**. All non-political judicial acts affecting private rights, commercial disputes, and ordinary crimes remain valid and enforceable after liberation. To wipe them out would destroy all civil property rights and produce legal chaos.

---

### 19. Sangalang v. Intermediate Appellate Court, 176 SCRA 719 (1989)
- **Ponente:** Justice Abraham Sarmiento
- **Core Subject:** Police Power vs. Non-Impairment of Contracts; Abatement of Nuisance.
- **Facts:** Makati Mayor Nemesio Yabut ordered the demolition of the security perimeter gate on Orbit Street erected by the Bel-Air Village Association (BAVA) to open the street to public vehicular traffic and relieve extreme congestion between Buendia and EDSA. BAVA sued, claiming a violation of the Non-Impairment Clause and an unconstitutional taking of private property without compensation.
- **Issue:** May a municipal mayor demolish a subdivision security gate under police power despite private restrictive deed covenants?
- **Ruling:** **YES.** The Mayor's action was valid.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  *Police Power Overrides Private Contracts:* Private contracts and restrictive covenants must yield to the superior sovereign exercise of police power. Police power cannot be bartered away. Severe traffic congestion is a public hazard; opening roads serves the general public welfare (*Salus populi est suprema lex*). Gates obstructing public circulation constitute public nuisances that may be abated.

---

### 20. Republic v. Ortigas and Company, 717 SCRA 601 (2014) & Republic v. PNB, 1 SCRA 957 (1961)
- **Core Subject:** Power of Eminent Domain; Measure of Just Compensation; The Point of Taking.
- **Facts:** The government expropriated private lands for public infrastructure. Landowners sought compensation evaluated at the conclusion of protracted litigation decades later, citing runaway inflation and increased commercial land values.
- **Issue:** What is the legal point of time for calculating the fair market value of expropriated property?
- **Ruling:** Just compensation must be measured **as of the date of actual physical taking or the date of filing the expropriation complaint**, whichever comes first.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  The measure of just compensation is the owner's loss at the time the property was taken, not the taker's subsequent gain, nor speculative inflation decades later. However, legal interest must be paid from the date of taking until full satisfaction to compensate for delayed payment.

---

### 21. Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Algue, Inc., 158 SCRA 9 (1988)
- **Ponente:** Justice Isagani Cruz
- **Core Subject:** The Lifeblood Theory of Taxation; The Symbiotic Relationship.
- **Facts:** Algue, Inc. claimed a deduction for promotional promotional fees paid to agents. The CIR disallowed the deduction as fictitious and assessed deficiency income taxes, invoking the power of taxation.
- **Issue:** Did the CIR properly disallow the legitimate business expense?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** The deduction was allowed.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Justice Cruz penned the immortal formulation of the **Lifeblood Theory**:
  > *"Taxes are what we pay for civilized society. Without taxes, the government would be paralyzed for lack of the motive power to activate and operate it. Hence, despite the natural reluctance to surrender part of one's hard-earned income to the taxing authorities, every person who lives to enjoy the freedom and protections of the State is bound to contribute his proportion toward the expenses of government."*
  However, taxation is not unbridled power; it must be exercised with fairness and reasonableness. Taxpayers are not to be treated as suspects.

---

### 22. Sanders v. Veridiano II, 162 SCRA 88 (1988)
- **Ponente:** Justice Isagani Cruz
- **Core Subject:** State Immunity from Suit; Ultra Vires Exception; Official vs. Personal Capacity.
- **Facts:** Sanders (a US military naval officer) and Moreau (a civilian director at Subic Naval Base) were sued for damages in a Philippine RTC by Filipino civilian employees for issuing an administrative memo that allegedly defamed them. Defendants invoked State Immunity.
- **Issue:** Does state immunity shield foreign military officials who act in their official capacity?
- **Ruling:** **YES.** The suit was dismissed.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  The doctrine of state immunity applies to foreign sovereign officials acting within the scope of their assigned official duties. A suit against them in their official capacity is in reality a suit against the foreign State, which cannot be entertained without its consent (*par in parem non habet imperium*). However, if an officer acts with personal malice, bad faith, or ultra vires, he is personally liable and loses state immunity.

---

### 23. Arigo v. Swift, 735 SCRA 102 (2014)
- **Ponente:** Justice Martin Villarama, Jr.
- **Core Subject:** Restrictive Theory of State Immunity; Acts Jure Imperii; The USS Guardian Grounding.
- **Facts:** The USS Guardian, an American naval warship, ran aground and damaged over 2,300 square meters of the protected coral reef of the Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park. Environmentalists filed a petition for a Writ of Kalikasan and sought civil damages from U.S. Navy officers (Commander Swift et al.).
- **Issue:** May officers of a foreign sovereign warship be sued for environmental damages in Philippine courts without sovereign consent?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** The suit against the US naval officers was dismissed.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Under the Restrictive Theory of State Immunity, warships are sovereign military assets. The operation, navigation, and transit of a military warship is an act **jure imperii** (governmental military act). Foreign military commanders acting in their official naval roles enjoy sovereign immunity. Any claim for damages must be settled through diplomatic and international state-to-state channels.

---

### 24. Kilosbayan, Incorporated v. Guingona, Jr., 232 SCRA 110 (1994)
- **Ponente:** Justice Hilario Davide, Jr.
- **Core Subject:** Constitutional Standing; Transcendental Importance; PCSO Lottery Contract.
- **Facts:** The PCSO entered into an on-line lottery "Contract of Lease" with Philippine Gaming Management Corporation (PGMC), a Malaysian-controlled entity. Kilosbayan (a civic group of senators, bishops, and citizens) challenged the contract as a violation of BP Blg. 42, which prohibited PCSO from holding lotteries in collaboration, association, or joint venture with any other entity.
- **Issue:** Does Kilosbayan have standing, and does the "Contract of Lease" violate the statutory prohibition?
- **Ruling:** **YES on both counts.** The lease contract was declared null and void.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  1. *Transcendental Importance:* In cases of profound public interest involving constitutional and statutory fidelity, procedural requirements of standing are relaxed.
  2. *Substance over Form:* The contract, though disguised as a "lease," gave PGMC full operational control and revenue sharing. It was a prohibited joint venture and association that circumvented legislative commands.

---

### 25. Manila Prince Hotel v. GSIS, 267 SCRA 408 (1997)
- **Ponente:** Justice Josue Bellosillo
- **Core Subject:** Constitutional Construction; Self-Executing Provisions; The Filipino First Policy.
- **Facts:** GSIS conducted bidding to privatize 51% of the shares of the historic Manila Hotel. Renong Berhad (a Malaysian firm) submitted the highest bid at Php 44.00 per share. Manila Prince Hotel (a Filipino corporation) bid Php 41.58. Before the award was finalized, Manila Prince Hotel matched Renong Berhad's bid of Php 44.00 and demanded the award pursuant to Section 10, Paragraph 2, Article XII of the 1987 Constitution (the **Filipino First Policy**). GSIS refused, arguing that the provision was non-self-executing and required an implementing statute.
- **Issue:** Is Section 10, Paragraph 2, Article XII of the Constitution self-executing, and does "national patrimony" encompass cultural and historical heritage?
- **Ruling:** **YES.** GSIS was ordered to award the shares to Manila Prince Hotel.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  1. *Presumption of Self-Executing Nature:* Constitutional provisions are presumed self-executing unless the text explicitly leaves implementation to the legislature.
  2. *National Patrimony Defined:* "National Patrimony" includes cultural and historical heritage. The Manila Hotel is an integral part of the nation's heritage.
  3. *The Filipino Matching Rule:* When a qualified Filipino corporation matches the highest bid of a foreign bidder in matters concerning the national economy and patrimony, the Filipino *must* be preferred.

---

### 26. People v. Veneracion, 249 SCRA 244 (1995)
- **Ponente:** Justice Santiago Kapunan
- **Core Subject:** Judicial Duty vs. Personal Moral Conscience; Separation of Powers; Capital Punishment.
- **Facts:** Accused were convicted of rape with homicide, a crime for which Republic Act No. 7659 prescribed the mandatory penalty of *death*. However, the trial judge, Judge Florentino Veneracion, refused to impose the death penalty, citing his deep personal religious convictions as a devout Catholic against capital punishment, and sentenced the accused to *reclusion perpetua*.
- **Issue:** May a judge refuse to impose a mandatory statutory penalty based on his religious beliefs or moral conscience?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** Judge Veneracion was ordered to impose the death penalty.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Judges cannot substitute their personal religious or moral beliefs for the clear mandate of positive law. Under separation of powers, the legislature prescribes penalties; the judiciary's sworn duty is to apply them. A judge who refuses to enforce positive law creates judicial anarchy.

---

### 27. Villavicencio v. Lukban, 39 Phil. 778 (1919)
- **Ponente:** Justice George Malcolm
- **Core Subject:** The Rule of Law vs. Government of Men; Habeas Corpus; Banishment of Prostitutes.
- **Facts:** Manila Mayor Justo Lukban and Police Chief Anton rounded up 170 prostitutes in the Gardenia district and, in the dead of night, without warrants, trial, or consent, deported them to Davao to work as agricultural laborers. A writ of habeas corpus was filed against the Mayor.
- **Issue:** Does a city mayor have legal authority to banish citizens without due process of law?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** Mayor Lukban was held in contempt and ordered to bring the women back.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  Justice Malcolm's timeless pronouncement:
  > *"The Philippines is a government of laws and not of men... No official, no matter how high, is above the law. Prostitutes, though unfortunate victims of human depravity, are nonetheless Filipino citizens possessing civil rights. No official has the authority to banish them or treat them as cattle without due process of law."*
  Habeas Corpus is the Great Writ of Liberty that strikes down arbitrary executive overreach.

---

### 28. Guido v. Rural Progress Administration, 84 Phil. 847 (1949)
- **Ponente:** Justice Pedro Tuason
- **Core Subject:** Social Justice; Limits of Eminent Domain; Small Landholdings.
- **Facts:** The Rural Progress Administration sought to expropriate Justina Guido's two adjoining lots in Caloocan (22,655 sq.m. commercial and residential land) under Commonwealth Act No. 539 to subdivide it into small home lots and sell them to the 60 current tenants. Guido objected, claiming her land was a modest commercial property, not a landed estate.
- **Issue:** May the State expropriate small private landholdings under the banner of social justice?
- **Ruling:** **NO.** The expropriation was nullified.
- **Jurisprudential Doctrine:**
  *The True Meaning of Social Justice:*
  Commonwealth Act No. 539 authorizes the expropriation of vast agrarian landed estates (*haciendas*), not small private lots. Taking land from one private owner to give it to another private person destroys property rights. Social justice does not mean equal division of property or forced economic equality; it guarantees **equality of opportunity, equality before the law, and fair treatment for all**.

# PART IV: HIGH-YIELD BAR EXAM Q&A & LATIN MAXIMS

### Essential Latin Maxims in Legal Philosophy
1. ***Salus Populi Est Suprema Lex*** — The welfare of the people is the supreme law. (Fundamental basis of Police Power).
2. ***Sic Utere Tuo Ut Alienum Non Laedas*** — Use your property so as not to injure the rights of another. (Regulates private property).
3. ***Verba Legis Non Est Recedendum*** — From the words of a statute there should be no departure. (Plain Meaning Rule).
4. ***Ratio Legis Est Anima Legis*** — The reason of the law is the soul of the law. (Intent prevails over literal text).
5. ***Par In Parem Non Habet Imperium*** — An equal has no power over an equal. (Foundation of State Immunity).
6. ***Pacta Sunt Servanda*** — Agreements must be kept. (Moral and natural law foundation of contracts).
7. ***Stare Decisis Et Non Quieta Movere*** — Stand by things decided and do not disturb settled points. (Doctrine of Precedent).
8. ***Ut Magis Valeat Quam Pereat*** — That the thing may rather have effect than perish. (Holistic constitutional construction).
9. ***Lex Iniusta Non Est Lex*** — An unjust law is no law at all. (Core natural law maxim).
10. ***Ignorantia Legis Neminem Excusat*** — Ignorance of the law excuses no one. (Civil Code Art. 3).

### Model Bar Examination Questions & Answers

#### Question 1: Letter vs. Spirit of the Law
**Q:** *A statute requires that notice of redemption among co-heirs be given "in writing." An estranged co-heir had actual knowledge of the sale for over 15 years, during which the buyer constructed a home on the land. The co-heir now seeks to exercise redemption, claiming the 30-day statutory period never ran because no written notice was served. How would you resolve the petition from the standpoint of legal philosophy?*  
**A:** I would dismiss the petition for redemption. While positive law mentions "notice in writing," legal philosophy recognizes that the spirit prevails over the letter (*ratio legis est anima legis*). As held in *Alonzo v. IAC* (150 SCRA 259), the legislative intent of the written notice requirement is simply to ensure actual awareness. To allow redemption after 15 years of actual knowledge would weaponize a protective statute into an instrument of extortion. The court must defer *"not to the letter that killeth, but to the spirit that vivifieth."*

#### Question 2: Legal Realism & Operative Fact
**Q:** *The President created several municipalities via Executive Order. Five years later, the Supreme Court declared the enabling law unconstitutional as an undue delegation of legislative power. Are all contracts, marriages, and official acts performed by the municipal officials during those five years null and void ab initio? Explain under Legal Realism.*  
**A:** **NO.** Under the **Doctrine of Operative Fact** enunciated in *Municipality of Malabang v. Benito* (27 SCRA 533), the law recognizes that prior to the judicial declaration of unconstitutionality, the statute was an operative historical reality. People relied upon it, contracts were executed, taxes collected, and public duties performed. Under legal realism, the past cannot be erased. The municipality acted as a de facto corporation, and its past official acts remain legally binding.

#### Question 3: Judicial Duty vs. Religious Conscience
**Q:** *A judge presides over a criminal case where the law mandates the imposition of capital punishment upon proof of guilt beyond reasonable doubt. The judge convicts the accused but imposes life imprisonment, stating that his religious conscience and natural law forbid him from taking a human life. Is the judge's action legally defensible?*  
**A:** **NO.** The judge's action violates the separation of powers. In *People v. Veneracion* (249 SCRA 244), the Supreme Court ruled that judges cannot substitute their personal religious beliefs or moral conscience for the positive penalties prescribed by the legislature. The legislature prescribes penalties; the judiciary is sworn to apply them. Allowing judges to disregard statutory penalties creates judicial anarchy.

#### Question 4: State Immunity & Restrictive Theory
**Q:** *A foreign naval warship visits Manila on a goodwill tour. While maneuvering in the harbor, it damages a commercial wharf. The wharf owner sues the foreign naval commander and the foreign State in a Philippine Regional Trial Court for damages. Will the suit prosper?*  
**A:** **NO.** Under the **Restrictive Theory of State Immunity** (*Arigo v. Swift*, 735 SCRA 102; *Sanders v. Veridiano II*, 162 SCRA 88), a foreign State and its military officers are immune from suit for acts **jure imperii** (sovereign/governmental acts). The navigation and transit of a sovereign military warship is an act *jure imperii*. The domestic court has no jurisdiction under the maxim *par in parem non habet imperium*. The wharf owner must pursue his remedy through diplomatic channels.

#### Question 5: Social Justice & Eminent Domain
**Q:** *Under the constitutional banner of Social Justice, the government seeks to expropriate a modest 1-hectare residential property owned by an elderly couple to subdivide it into 20 lots for indigent informal settlers. The couple resists, arguing the property is their sole life savings. Decide with jurisprudential basis.*  
**A:** The expropriation must be denied. As held in *Guido v. Rural Progress Administration* (84 Phil. 847), Social Justice does not champion the equal division of property or the arbitrary confiscation of small private holdings to benefit other private individuals. Social Justice is equality of opportunity and fair treatment; it targets vast agrarian estates (*haciendas*) that cause feudal oppression. Atomizing modest private property under the guise of social justice destroys individual property security.
