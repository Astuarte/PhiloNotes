# CHAPTER 1: PRELIMINARY CONSIDERATIONS

## 1. Philosophy and the Law

### Main idea

The book begins by establishing a **natural relationship between philosophy and law**. Philosophy is concerned with inquiry, reasoning, and understanding, while law is concerned with order, rules, and regulation.

The author explains that philosophy can help law students understand:

- the **nature of law**
- its **concept**
- its **application**
- why people are required to obey it
- how laws relate to **justice and equity**
Philosophy allows us to go beyond simply memorizing statutes. It encourages us to ask **why a law exists, what it is intended to accomplish, and whether its application produces justice**.

### ⭐ Important point

**Law provides rules; philosophy helps us understand the reason and principles behind those rules.**

The relationship is therefore not merely theoretical. Philosophical thinking can help lawyers and judges examine laws more critically and understand their broader social and human implications.

## 2. Nature of Philosophy

### Main idea

The book describes philosophy as **inquisitive by nature**.

Philosophy constantly asks questions and searches for answers. It challenges what people normally accept and encourages them to look beyond what they have already been taught.

### Important concepts

**Philosophy = inquiry.**

It involves:

- questioning
- reasoning
- examining assumptions
- searching for truth
- challenging established ideas
- looking deeper into concepts
The author emphasizes that philosophy helps people look **further than what they have been taught**.

### ⭐ Remember:

**Philosophy does not simply accept. It asks “why?”**

This is particularly important in law because legal rules may have different interpretations depending on their purpose, context, and application.

## 3. Reason and Spirit of the Law

This is one of the **most important concepts in Chapter 1**.

The chapter distinguishes between the **letter of the law** and the **spirit of the law**.

**Letter of the law**

This refers to the **literal wording** of a legal provision.

**Spirit of the law**

This refers to the **purpose, intent, meaning, or reason behind the law**.

The book emphasizes that courts should not mechanically apply legal provisions when doing so would defeat the purpose of the law. The interpretation of law must consider its underlying intent.

A key principle highlighted in the chapter is:

**“The spirit, rather than the letter of a statute determines its construction.”**

### ⭐ Very important distinction

| Letter of the Law | Spirit of the Law |
| --- | --- |
| Literal wording | Purpose or intent |
| What the law says | What the law seeks to accomplish |
| Text | Meaning |
| Strict application | Application consistent with purpose |

### Example from the chapter

The book discusses cases where the Supreme Court considered whether a **strict reading of the law** would defeat the purpose intended by the legislature.

The discussion emphasizes that judges are not simply machines that apply words mechanically. They must consider the **purpose of the law**, while still respecting the limits of judicial interpretation.

### ⭐ Key principle to memorize

**Law and justice should be interpreted in harmony.**

The chapter specifically stresses that law should be applied **in consonance with justice**, rather than independently of it.

## 4. Brief History of Law

### Main idea

The chapter provides a short historical discussion to explain how law developed and how Philippine law was influenced by different legal traditions.

The author points out the strong influence of the **American legal system** on Philippine constitutional and political institutions.

### Important historical development

The chapter refers to the evolution of law from:

- early Roman law
- Roman law's development and codification
- common law traditions
- modern legal systems
- Philippine law under American influence
One important observation is that **law develops together with society**. As society changes, legal rules and institutions also evolve.

**Philippine context**

The chapter discusses how Philippine political and legal institutions were significantly influenced by the United States, including:

- democratic institutions
- constitutional government
- Bill of Rights concepts
- separation of powers
- protection of individual rights
At the same time, the discussion emphasizes that Filipinos have their own history and traditions concerning **freedom, rights, and government**.

### ⭐ Important idea

**Law is not isolated from history.**

Understanding the historical development of a legal system helps explain why its present institutions and principles exist.

## 5. Structure of Our Legal System

The chapter then shifts from philosophy and history to the **Philippine governmental structure**.

The Philippine political structure is composed of **three coordinate branches of government**:

- **Executive**
- **Legislative**
- **Judiciary**
The three branches are intended to operate as **separate but coordinated branches**, with checks and balances preventing any one branch from becoming completely dominant.

### ⭐ Basic division

| Branch | Basic Function |
| --- | --- |
| Executive | Applies/executes the law |
| Legislative | Makes the law |
| Judiciary | Interprets the law |

This is one of the easiest and most important things to memorize from Chapter 1.

## 6. The Executive

### Main function:

**APPLIES THE LAW**

The Executive branch is headed by the **President**.

The chapter explains that the President:

- exercises executive power
- serves as Chief Executive
- acts as head of the executive departments
- ensures that laws are faithfully executed
- exercises administrative and executive functions through the executive departments and agencies
The chapter specifically describes the Executive as the branch that **“applies the law.”**

### ⭐ Remember:

**Executive = Executes/Applies the law**

A major constitutional principle discussed is that acts performed by executive and administrative agencies in carrying out executive functions are generally considered acts of the Executive.

## 7. The Legislative

### Main function:

**MAKES THE LAW**

The Legislative branch is composed of **Congress**, which has two chambers:

- **Senate**
- **House of Representatives**
The chapter explains that Congress possesses legislative authority to:

- enact laws
- amend laws
- repeal laws
The Constitution also places limitations on legislative power. Congress cannot simply exercise unlimited authority.

### ⭐ Remember:

**Legislative = Makes the law**

### Important point

The legislative function is primarily the **prescription of general rules for the government of society**.

The chapter also notes that Congress has constitutional limitations, including limitations concerning what conduct may be declared criminal and what punishments may be prescribed.

## 8. The Judiciary

### Main function:

**INTERPRETS THE LAW**

The Judiciary is represented by the courts, with the **Supreme Court** at the top.

The chapter emphasizes that the Judiciary:

- interprets laws
- decides cases and controversies
- determines legal rights
- resolves conflicts involving governmental authority
- exercises judicial power
The book describes the Judiciary as the branch that **“interprets the law.”**

### Judicial Power

This is an especially important concept.

Under the 1987 Constitution, judicial power includes the authority to:

- **settle actual controversies involving rights that are legally demandable and enforceable**, and
- determine whether there has been a **grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction** by any branch or instrumentality of government.
### ⭐ Important term:

**Judicial supremacy**

The Judiciary has the constitutional authority to determine whether governmental acts conform to the Constitution.

## 9. Judicial Review

Another major concept in this section is **judicial review**.

### Definition

Judicial review is the power of the courts to examine the validity of governmental acts and determine whether they conform to the Constitution and applicable law.

The chapter explains that judicial review tests the validity of legislative or executive acts through an appropriate legal proceeding.

**Requirements discussed in the chapter**

The book discusses requirements for judicial review, including:

- an **actual and appropriate case or controversy**
- a party with the necessary **personal and substantial interest**
- the exercise of judicial review being raised at the proper time
- the constitutional question being raised at the earliest opportunity
- the constitutional question being the **very lis mota of the case**
### ⭐ Very important:

**Lis mota** = the constitutional issue must be the **very issue that determines the outcome of the case**.

## 10. Justiciable Controversy

The chapter also introduces the concept of a **justiciable controversy**.

### Meaning

A justiciable controversy involves a **definite and concrete dispute** between parties having adverse legal interests.

In other words, courts do not decide purely hypothetical questions. There must be an actual legal dispute appropriate for judicial determination.

### ⭐ Remember:

**Justiciable controversy = actual, concrete legal dispute suitable for judicial resolution.**

## 11. Constitutional Commissions

The 1987 Philippine Constitution created **three independent constitutional commissions**:

- **Civil Service Commission (CSC)**
- **Commission on Elections (COMELEC)**
- **Commission on Audit (COA)**
Their independence is important because they perform constitutional functions that require freedom from improper interference.

### ⭐ Memorize:

**CSC + COMELEC + COA = Three Constitutional Commissions**

## 12. Civil Service Commission (CSC)

### Main function:

The CSC serves as the **central personnel agency of the Philippine government**.

It covers government branches, subdivisions, instrumentalities, and agencies, including government-owned or controlled corporations with original charters.

**Functions/concerns discussed:**

- civil service administration
- personnel matters
- merit and fitness
- career service
- professionalism
- efficiency
- integrity
- responsiveness
- accountability
The Constitution requires measures to promote **morale, efficiency, integrity, responsiveness, progressiveness, and courtesy in the civil service**.

### ⭐ Important principle:

**Public office is a public trust.**

Appointments in the civil service should generally be based on **merit and fitness**, subject to applicable legal requirements.

## 13. Commission on Elections (COMELEC)

### Main function:

The COMELEC is an **independent constitutional commission responsible for administering elections**.

The chapter emphasizes its role in ensuring:

- free elections
- orderly elections
- honest elections
- peaceful elections
- credible elections
It is described as a guardian of the **sacred right of suffrage**.

### Important concept: Independence

COMELEC is constitutionally independent, but its acts may still be subject to **judicial review** when appropriate.

The chapter discusses the constitutional authority of COMELEC over election-related matters and the limits of judicial intervention.

### ⭐ Remember:

**COMELEC = Elections + Suffrage**

## 14. Commission on Audit (COA)

### Main function:

**Audits government funds and expenditures.**

The COA is the government's constitutional **watchdog over public funds**.

The chapter explains that COA is empowered to determine whether government entities comply with laws and regulations involving the **disbursement of government funds**. It may also disallow illegal or irregular expenditures.

### Important powers

COA has authority to:

- examine government accounts
- audit expenditures
- examine government funds
- determine compliance with laws and regulations
- disallow illegal or irregular disbursements
- conduct post-audits
- audit certain non-governmental entities receiving government subsidies or equity
The chapter describes COA as the **central auditor of the government** and connects its powers with the constitutional system of **checks and balances**.

### ⭐ Remember:

**COA = Government funds + auditing + accountability**
